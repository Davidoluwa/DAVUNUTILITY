How the council took the 40-question exam

Four members sit on the council. Two are symbolic (squares) and two are neural (circles). Nobody wrote rules for who answers what. A router and a judge, both trained on the practice questions, decide whom to ask and which answer to trust. Pick a question to see how it was read, what each member privately worked out, whom the router asked, what the reviewers reported and how the judge weighed it. The council was trained on the curriculum only and frozen for the exam.

How to read the numbers

Router: the learned chance that a member is right on this kind of question, read from the question's words and shape. Members are asked most promising first, and the next one only if a second opinion is worth its compute. Self-confidence is the member's own estimate, from its track record in similar situations. Reviewer reports are signals, never vetoes: the Checker says which checks pass or fail and whether it trusts each one here, and the Estimator says how close an answer is to its guess of the size. The judge weighs every candidate and a "none of these" option. Members who agree pool their weight. An answer is delivered only if its probability clears the line (0.5: a wrong answer costs as much as a right one earns). The reasons listed are the judge's learned weights that pushed hardest, with their sign.
